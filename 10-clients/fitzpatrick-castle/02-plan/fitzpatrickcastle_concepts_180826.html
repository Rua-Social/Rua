The direction

Where these are heading.

Everything here moves away from friendly, safe and stock, towards three feelings. Each piece should land at least one of them. If a piece doesn't, it isn't for us.

Curiosity

A reason to stop scrolling. The small surprise, the thing people didn't know was here.

Charm

Real people, lovely to be around. Scripted enough to hold, loose enough for the unplanned moment.

Grandeur

The castle, the heritage, the scale. Shown, never announced. We earn the name.

How to read this

Nine live concepts, grouped by how much they need on the day, lightest first. Some are single pieces, some are series or banks that yield several. Green means ready to shoot. Amber means one thing is still open, usually a name, a date, or a stop. 007 was retired on 18 August. That number is not reused.

The number

Counted conservatively, the nine live concepts yield around twenty-one finished pieces, comfortably past the floor of twelve, and more again once the longer pieces get clipped into shorts. Repeatable formats are doing that work, not volume for its own sake. If the 004 bubbles swap is confirmed, that bank yields one more.

Anatomy Of

One thing being made or served, captured close and in real time, narrated by whoever's doing it. No wide shots, no styling, no soft music. The detail is the point and the voice carries it. The format runs across as many subjects as the property offers. Three are ready to shoot now.

Why it works

This is the strongest instinct in your own data. The pastry chef piece was one of only two things that actually performed. Real, close, unstyled and voiced beats every polished plate on the channel. Because it's a format, each new subject is another finished piece with no new thinking required.

The Coffee Hatch

Drive-thru coffee without leaving the car, which is a genuinely unusual detail for a hotel like this. Iced drinks, latte art, the specialty coffee feel, sitting next to paddle and the outdoors as part of the casual, active side of the place.

Why it works

Curiosity does the work here. A castle hotel with a coffee hatch is a small surprise worth showing, and it reads younger than the rest of the channel. The still vignettes slow it down and give the charm somewhere to live, so the piece hits the feelings rather than just the offer.

Who

One person on the hatch with a bit of pep, not a flat read.

Where

The hatch, then the quiet spots around it: the garden, the padel courts.

When

Shoot day, minimal setup, worked in around the bigger pieces.

The shape of it

  • 01The hand-off. Cars pulling up, the latte art, the pass through the hatch. Quick and bright.
  • 01bSame hatch, glass of bubbles. Same hands, same height. A pre-poured glass goes through the hatch the way the cup does. No pour, no garnish.
  • 02This. Static, slow zoom: the cup on a table, the garden behind it.
  • 02bThen this. Same table, same zoom, glass on the cup's mark.
  • 03And this. Static, slow zoom: a padel rally mid-game, the sound of play kept in. One take. Do not double this as a drinks shot.
  • 04And this. One more still moment found on the day, the castle or the terrace behind.
Format note

Opens quick and candid at the hatch, then the pace drops into a run of static, slow-zoom vignettes, each introduced with two words of on-screen text: this, and this. The stillness is where the charm lands and the castle setting earns the curiosity. Scored, light on dialogue. Coffee and bubbles share the same marks, so one setup yields two cuts. Internal slates 004-coffee and 004-bubbles. This is not the Library Bar craft piece; that stays 003C.

References

The Rabbit · the pace and tone, pointed at the hatch, the terrace and the paddle court rather than the wider brand.

A View From A Castle

Long-serving, customer-facing staff telling a real story. Two treatments: the signature pull-back, where the subject sits small and distant in frame and the shot slowly reveals them in the full context of the hotel, and a mic on the subject mid-service, camera static, the room's hustle carrying the frame. Each episode is a ninety-second vignette. Three or four in the series, and the lead cut goes to the longest-serving name in the building, a real number in the opening line.

Why it works

People make the place, and your data proves it. The moments that landed were real people, not stock, and the post marking John Fitzgerald's retirement drew engagement the channel rarely sees. These are Dubliners who have been here decades and talk to guests every day. The charm you promise at the desk is the charm this captures on camera.

Episode 1 Sandra Jay

Senior executive manager, here forty-odd years. A natural anchor for the series.

Episode 2 Alan

The porter who runs the front and makes every arrival easy. Suits the mic'd service treatment, pottering the morning shift, or the pull-back to a full portrait of the hotel front.

Episode 3 James

On reception, and the example Alicia reaches for herself: he remembers a guest's name five minutes after check-in.

Episode 4 · Wildcard Tommy

The bookend. The newest person in the building against the forty-year servers: kitchen porter, then the bar, then a design degree, now the group's social.

Prompts

  • Who's the guest you still remember, years later?
  • What's changed here, and what's exactly the same?
  • What would you tell someone about this place that they'd never guess?
