# Research analysis

How to read a client's social history at Stage 1, and how to turn it into a concept long-list.

Research is generative, not background. The point is not a tidy audit. The point is to come out with a
clear picture of what lands with this audience, what does not, and what is missing, and a rough set of
concepts that picture has generated.

---

## What to ingest

Six months of the client's own social history is the mandatory minimum. Pull the post-level export
(date, format, caption, likes, any saved or shared counts available) plus a visual scroll of the feed
itself. Numbers tell you what performed. The scroll tells you why it looks the way it does.

For a client with little history, flag the gap openly and supplement: competitor accounts, the wider
category, and any brief material the client has shared. Do not skip the read and jump to ideas.

---

## What to look for

Work through these. They are the lenses that produced the useful findings on Skehans.

**Format split.** Video versus static, Reels versus in-feed. Establish which format the account
actually rewards, separate from which it posts most. On Skehans the account published mostly static but
video was rewarded far more heavily, which set the whole direction toward more video volume.

**Content category.** People, product, or place. Which subjects recur, and which ones outperform. On
Skehans the strongest pillars came out as the hosts, the regulars, the community, and Guinness.

**Baseline, not peaks.** Find the average of a normal good post once outliers are stripped. On Skehans
that baseline sat around 200 likes, which is the number every later concept was measured against. Do
not let a viral post set the bar.

**Outliers, named as outliers.** Identify what spiked and decide whether it is a repeatable pillar or a
one-off. On Skehans, music content (The Pogues, The Scratch, choirs) averaged far above baseline but
was treated as an unplanned viral opportunity, not a core category to manufacture. Calling an outlier
an outlier is part of the job.

**Sponsored and assisted content, flagged separately.** Anything brand-assisted or paid is removed from
the organic read so it does not skew the baseline. On Skehans a Jameson-linked post was pulled from the
general metrics for exactly this reason. Note it, do not count it as organic proof.

**Caption and tone.** What voice lands. Match what is good and lift what is flat. The copy voice you
carry into the concepts deck comes from here.

**Frequency and consistency.** How often they post and how steady it is. This sets expectations for how
the sprint output gets dripped out afterward.

---

## The output of this stage

Two things, both your own working material at this point:

1. A findings summary, structured around the lenses above. This is what becomes the Week 0 deck at
   Stage 2.
2. A rough concept long-list. Hold it loosely. It is the raw material for the greenlight call, where
   the client steers, cuts, and adds. Do not present it as fixed.

Read `references/creative-library.md` while you work, for what has worked, what has not, and what got
corrected on past sprints.
