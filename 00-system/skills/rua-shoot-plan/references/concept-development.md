# Concept development

Concept types, tier logic, pillar thinking, and the anatomy of a full treatment. Used at Stage 3 (the
greenlight call) and Stage 4 (building the concepts deck).

---

## Pillars

Concepts hang off a small set of pillars drawn from the research. Each concept should hit one pillar
cleanly, two at a stretch. Three is too much to carry in a single short asset.

The Skehans pillars, as an example of the shape:
- **People**, split into staff and customers.
- **Place**, the building inside and out.
- **Community**, less about the pub itself and more about how it sits at the centre of its
  neighbourhood and the Irish community around it.
- **Guinness**, which folds neatly into people and place without much effort.

A new client gets its own pillars from its own research. The method carries, the pillars do not.

## Concept types

Name the type for every concept. It drives how many assets come out and how it is scheduled.

- **Standalone.** One concept, one deliverable.
- **Mini-series.** An episodic arc across several posts, built from one shoot or returned to over time.
  Skehans examples: A View from Skehans (Bryan, Douglas, Natasha as episodes), the Julius arc, the Chai
  arc, the Memorabilia Walk.
- **Recurring.** A format that repeats over time as a regular slot.
- **Asset bank.** A single setup that generates a cluster of short assets. Skehans example: the
  Telegraph Hill exterior, shot once to yield several pieces. The pint competition was treated as a
  bank of matchups in the same way.
- **Sub-concepts.** Variations inside one concept, numbered under it (for example 007 Sub A, Sub B, Sub
  C). Useful when one idea spawns close cousins that share a setup.

Flag the type at the greenlight call so the client understands that ten green-lit concepts can yield
fifteen or more assets, and that a bank or a series does not need finishing in one go.

## Tier logic

Order concepts by who needs to be there on the day. Lightest logistics first. Each tier adds a person
or a coordination track. This ordering runs through the overview table in the concepts deck, the block
order in the schedule, and the shot list.

The logic, lightest to heaviest:
- Concepts needing nobody (exteriors, empty interiors, B-roll banks).
- Concepts needing one staff member already on site.
- Concepts needing a specific named subject (the landlord, a named regular, the kitchen owner).
- Concepts needing several people coordinated into one window.

Ordering this way means the day builds from low-friction captures into the pieces that need the most
arranging, and it makes plain to the client exactly where their coordination is required.

## Treatment anatomy

Every concept page in the deck carries the same parts. Do not leave gaps. The full CSS and page markup
are in `references/document-build.md`; this is what each part is for.

- **Tier banner.** Which tier the concept sits in, across the top of the page.
- **Concept badges.** The number, the green or amber status, and the type (series, recurring,
  elevated). Numbers are permanent (see the concept-numbering rule in SKILL.md).
- **Title.** A real creative title, not the literal concept name.
- **Description.** What it captures and how it plays as a piece.
- **Insight box ("why it works").** The reason this earns a place, tied back to the research. This is
  where the pillar and the baseline thinking show up.
- **Logistics grid.** Who, where, when, in three cells. The practical shape of the capture.
- **Shot list**, where the concept needs one. The specific captures.
- **Question list**, where the concept is voiced. The questions to ask on the day.
- **Caption or format note.** How it is cut and captioned, plus the caption tag where relevant.

For a mini-series, add episode cards laying out each episode. For an asset bank, list the locked shots.
For a voiced interview concept, write open questions that invite a real answer rather than a flat one.
The Skehans rule of thumb: ask "who would you bring here to show them what London is really like"
rather than "what do you love about this pub". The first gets a story, the second gets a slogan.

## The copy bank

The concepts deck ends with a copy bank covering every concept, with on-screen text and post copy for
each, and no gaps. Captions are six words or fewer. Copy is pulled from real lines where they exist
already, and marked as a placeholder where the line will only come from the footage. Never invent a
quote and attribute it to a person. The standing no-politics, no-religion rule applies to everything in
the bank.

---

## At the greenlight call

Propose the list, grouped by tier. Categorise each concept live: green-lit, series, recurring or bank,
or killed. Kill freely and replace with something stronger. Keep the initial count generous if the
client wants the comfort of volume, on the understanding that some will be refined or merged later. Log
every kill so it carries into the change log when the deck is built.
