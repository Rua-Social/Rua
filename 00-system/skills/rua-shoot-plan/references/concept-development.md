# Concept development

Concept types, tier logic, pillar thinking, and the anatomy of a full
treatment. Used at Stage 3 (greenlight) and Stage 4 (the concepts deck).

---

## Pillars

Concepts hang off a small set of pillars drawn from this research. Each
concept should hit one pillar cleanly, two at a stretch. Three is too much
to carry in a single short asset.

Pillars are outputs of the read. They do not travel from the last job.
People / place / community is a common hospitality shape, not a default.

---

## Concept types

Name the type for every concept. It drives how many assets come out and how
it is scheduled.

- **Standalone.** One concept, one deliverable.
- **Mini-series.** An episodic arc across several posts, built from one shoot
  or returned to over time.
- **Recurring.** A format that repeats over time as a regular slot.
- **Asset bank.** A single setup that generates a cluster of short assets.
- **Sub-concepts.** Variations inside one concept, numbered under it
  (007 Sub A, Sub B, Sub C). Useful when one idea spawns close cousins that
  share a setup.

Flag the type at the greenlight call so the client understands that a short
list of green-lit concepts can yield more assets than the list length, and
that a bank or a series does not need finishing in one go.

---

## Tier logic

Order concepts by who needs to be there on the day. Lightest logistics first.
Each tier adds a person or a coordination track. This ordering runs through
the overview table, the block order in the schedule, and the shot list.

Lightest to heaviest:

- Nobody (exteriors, empty interiors, B-roll banks).
- One person already on site.
- A specific named subject who must be arranged.
- Several people coordinated into one window.

The day builds from low-friction captures into the pieces that need the most
arranging. The client can see exactly where their coordination is required.

## Treatment anatomy

Every concept page carries the same parts. Do not leave gaps. Markup lives in
`document-build.md`. This is what each part is for.

- **Tier banner.** Which tier, across the top.
- **Concept badges.** Number, green or amber status, type. Numbers are
  permanent (see SKILL.md).
- **Title.** A real creative title, not the literal concept name.
- **Description.** What it captures and how it plays.
- **Insight box ("why it works").** Tied back to this research. This is
  where the pillar and the baseline show up.
- **Logistics grid.** Who, where, when.
- **Shot list**, where the concept needs one.
- **Question list**, where the concept is voiced.
- **Caption or format note.** How it is cut and captioned.

**References block (optional).** A short list of analogue posts or films,
each naming the device it demonstrates, and verified against the source.
Use when the client needs to see what the piece should feel like. Do not
paste unexplained links.

For a mini-series, add episode cards. For an asset bank, list the locked
shots. For a voiced concept, write open questions that invite a story
rather than a slogan. Ask the question that gets a specific memory or a
choice, not the question that gets a marketing line.

---

## The copy bank

The concepts deck ends with a copy bank covering every concept, with
on-screen text and post copy, and no gaps. Copy is pulled from real lines
where they exist already, and marked as a placeholder where the line will
only come from the footage. Never invent a quote and attribute it to a
person.

Caption length and any editorial limits come from this client. Do not
import another job's caption law or taboo.

---

## At the greenlight call

Propose the list, grouped by tier. Categorise each concept live: green-lit,
series, recurring or bank, or killed. Kill freely and replace with something
stronger. Keep the initial count generous if the client wants the comfort of
volume, on the understanding that some will be refined or merged later. Log
every kill so it carries into the change log.
