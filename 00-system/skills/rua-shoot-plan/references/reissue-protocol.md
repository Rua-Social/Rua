# Pre-production and the reissue protocol

Stage 5. Creative changes that arrive after the concepts deck is approved
flow back through the concepts deck before anything downstream is rebuilt.
The schedule is never the home for a creative correction.

---

## The pre-production call

Held before the shoot, with the client. It locks the day's logistics and the
shape of the schedule. It will also, almost every time, surface creative
changes: named locations, refined questions, talent confirmed or swapped,
copy and framing adjustments.

Plus pure logistics: drop-off time, block timings, light windows, access.

---

## The audit

After the call, sort every change into one of two buckets.

**Touches creative.** Questions, talent, locations, copy, framing, anything
that changes what a concept is or how it plays. These trigger a concepts
deck reissue.

**Pure logistics.** Timings, drop-off, access windows, order of the day.
These belong in the schedule and do not need a concepts reissue on their
own.

If anything is ambiguous, treat it as creative. The cost of an extra
iteration is small. The cost of a creative change living only in the
schedule is a broken source of truth.

---

## The loop

1. Pre-pro call. Capture all changes.
2. Audit. Sort creative from logistics.
3. Concepts deck reissue. New dated iteration, change log updated. This
   file becomes canonical.
4. Schedule reissue. Rebuild from the new concepts deck, folding in
   logistics.
5. Shot list update. Rebuild from the new schedule.

Do not shortcut from step 1 to step 4.

---

## Release forms

Adopted at this stage, not improvised on the day. Issue a simple staff
release template to the client so it is ready before the schedule goes out.

Keep it plain: who is being filmed, that they consent to the footage being
used on the channels named in the scope, name, signature, date. The aim is
a clean permission trail with no friction.

If anyone filmed has not signed, hold that asset for client review before
any publish decision. If an unplanned interview strays into a topic this
client has ruled out, hold it the same way. Do not import another client's
editorial taboos as a standing Rua rule.

---

## What goes back to the client

Alongside the reissued schedule: a short named-subjects list (the people
they need to confirm, and any venue prep) and the release template. Keep it
to what they own and need to act on. Surface it once. Beyond flagging the
list and the form, this stage does not chase them.
