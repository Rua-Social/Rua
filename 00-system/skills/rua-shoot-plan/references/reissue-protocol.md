# Pre-production and the reissue protocol

Stage 5. This stage exists to protect one rule: creative changes that arrive after the concepts deck is
approved flow back through the concepts deck before anything downstream is rebuilt. The schedule is
never the home for a creative correction.

This was the gap in the original process. On Skehans, the 12 May pre-production call generated real
creative changes, and the clean way to absorb them was a new concepts iteration first, then a schedule,
then a shot list (the 25 May set). That is now the protocol.

---

## The pre-production call

Held before the shoot, with the client. It locks the day's logistics and the shape of the schedule. It
will also, almost every time, surface creative changes. On the Skehans call these included:

- Locations named (the upper space settled as "The View" for privacy).
- Questions refined (Julius and Chai question sets reworked).
- Talent confirmed or swapped (Julius confirmed, a replacement regular lined up if the first did not
  confirm).
- Copy and framing adjustments tied to specific concepts.

Plus pure logistics that do not touch creative: drop-off time, block timings, golden-hour windows.

---

## The audit

After the call, sort every change into one of two buckets.

**Touches creative.** Questions, talent, locations, copy, framing, anything that changes what a concept
is or how it plays. These cannot go straight into the schedule. They trigger a concepts deck reissue.

**Pure logistics.** Timings, drop-off, access windows, order of the day. These belong in the schedule
and do not need a concepts reissue on their own.

If anything is ambiguous, treat it as creative and run it through the concepts deck. The cost of an
extra iteration is small. The cost of a creative change living only in the schedule is a broken source
of truth.

---

## The loop

In order, every time:

1. **Pre-pro call.** Capture all changes.
2. **Audit.** Sort creative from logistics, as above.
3. **Concepts deck reissue.** Build a new dated concepts iteration carrying the creative changes. Add
   them to the change log. This new file becomes canonical.
4. **Schedule reissue.** Rebuild the schedule from the reissued concepts deck, folding in the logistics
   changes as well.
5. **Shot list update.** Rebuild the shot list from the reissued schedule.

Do not shortcut from step 1 to step 4. The concepts deck is rebuilt first or the chain breaks.

---

## Release forms

Release forms are adopted at this stage, not improvised on the day. On Skehans the call agreed mandatory
release forms for all staff on camera, and the action was to send the client a simple template before
the shoot.

Issue a simple staff release template to the client here. Keep it plain: who is being filmed, that they
consent to the footage being used on the client's social channels, name, signature, date. The aim is a
clean permission trail with no friction, ready before the schedule goes out rather than chased
afterward.

Consent also intersects with the standing no-politics, no-religion rule. If an unplanned interview
strays into either, or if anyone filmed has not signed, that asset is held for client review before any
publish decision. On Skehans the parish priest interview is the live example: it sits behind a client
review on both the religion rule and consent before it goes anywhere.

---

## What goes back to the client

Alongside the reissued schedule, the client gets a short named-subjects list (the people they need to
confirm and any venue prep) and the release template. Keep it to what they own and need to act on.
Surface it once. Talent and venue prep are theirs, and beyond flagging the list and the release form,
this stage does not chase them.
