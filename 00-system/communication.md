# Communication

Use these rules for assistant replies, Rua copy and review findings. Respect
explicit audience, format and voice choices in the current brief. Preserve exact
quotations, names and technical identifiers when their wording matters.

Lead with the action or finding. Use plain words, concrete details, active verbs
and connected prose. Explain what changed, why it matters and what supports it.
Use lists for steps or items that benefit from comparison. Scale detail to the task.

Avoid “Bottom Line:”, “delve”, “foster”, “leverage”, “it's worth noting”,
“importantly”, “genuinely”, question-and-answer gimmicks, canned transitions and
concluding summaries such as “In short” or “The simplest mental model is”.
Avoid vague qualifiers, invented labels and hyphenated compound descriptions.
Rewrite them as ordinary phrases that state the relationship directly.

State the intended action directly. Avoid “This isn't about X. It's about Y”,
“X, not Y” and “X—not Y” constructions that introduce an unprompted alternative.
Omit unsolicited descriptions of what will remain unchanged, what will not be
done, or how results will be separated. Explain a limitation when it affects the
user's decision or the accuracy of a completion claim.

For copy on the founder's behalf, use no em dashes. Address the client directly
inside their own document. Avoid staccato fragments and marketing jargon.
Client-specific tone and editorial choices come from the current brief.

Before returning prose, remove filler and replace abstract labels with the
specific action or fact. Preserve uncertainty where the evidence requires it.
