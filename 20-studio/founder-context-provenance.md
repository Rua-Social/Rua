# Founder model: Darragh, Rua Social

> `founder-context.md` is the current operational distillation of this richer founder-discovery record. Consult this document when deeper founder-specific evidence, uncertainty or provenance materially affects a decision, or when the baseline itself is being reconsidered.

Built from three discovery batches plus one verification pass. What an AI working inside Rua should understand about its founder, and what it should not assume.

## Level 1. Confirmed, directly stated

### What Rua is

Owner-operated. One person. Sean Browne, Percy Chamburaka and Marion Tremoy are brought in per job for shooting, editing, graphic design and related work. They are not employees and not a permanent team. Nobody depends on the business. Benjamin Aguilera was specific to the Otel work and is not part of the current shape.

### What Rua is for

Stated as a correction to any growth reading: he has not been deliberately building an agency. Rua has largely been taking referred work and getting better at doing it. The desired business should not be inferred to be a conventional growth agency.

The target is a deliberately lean, high-value creative business producing strong income and wealth-building capacity without maximising headcount, client count or working hours.

The purpose sharpened across the discovery and landed here, in his words: not simply a business that funds music from a distance, but one where **an increasing proportion of Rua's paid work overlaps with the creative work he would choose to spend his creative energy on anyway.** He explicitly asked for this to be retained as founder intent and tested, not converted into a service line, a skill or a positioning decision.

He also stated a broader basis for judging future work than revenue alone: the economics, whether he likes the client and the relationship, and whether the work develops or overlaps with the craft he wants to spend time on. He was clear this is intent, not a scoring system to be built.

### The business he does not want

Not a large agency. Not headcount growth. Not maximised working hours. Not a large roster of lower-value clients. Long term he is drawn to a small number of clients he genuinely enjoys, producing work he is proud of, at economics that make the relationship worthwhile. He named Kasia at Skehans as the model for that.

### Money, wealth and lifestyle

Roughly €3,000 to €4,000 a month is his current personal comfort requirement covering lifestyle, bills and savings. Since Otel ended he has been running at around €2,500 a month and has been comfortable, but does not want to remain there. He has an up-to-date spreadsheet with the real financial information.

He has explicitly stated there is no calculated Rua revenue target. The €3,000 to €4,000 is a personal comfort figure and should not be treated as a modelled business number.

What the money is for: working relatively smart hours, making good money, building and maintaining wealth, renovating and potentially upgrading the house, cash reserves, investing, affording things like a good car, feeling financially secure enough to think about children. Alongside that, time to play guitar, go for coffee, eat out, and live a creative life rather than optimise himself around work.

Long term, one or two good shoots or engagements a month could suit him better than maximising client volume, provided the economics work. Stated as a possibility he finds attractive, not as a plan.

### Music and creative ambition

Papa Rua is his artist project. His creativity and music are, in his words, extremely important to him. He ran a substantial public campaign for Overthinkin from roughly January to April, which had a measurable positive effect on his Spotify figures, and he holds the underlying Spotify for Artists and Instagram data. Next release 21 August. He is not particularly worried about the music project at present.

What genuinely excites him is making music videos and developing that craft properly: planning them, making creative decisions in advance, lighting them properly, shooting deliberately, getting more decisions right before the edit, and developing a strong visual identity.

He would love to make his own music videos really well, and the idea that he could eventually make music videos and music-related content for other artists as meaningful paid work is close to a dream version of what he could be doing professionally.

### The relationship between Rua and Papa Rua

Not a firewall and not a funding arrangement at arm's length. He explicitly corrected the "Rua efficiently makes money so music happens elsewhere" reading.

He identified that a properly planned and executed Papa Rua music video can simultaneously be an artistic output for Papa Rua, deliberate practice in the filmmaking craft he wants to improve, and proof or portfolio material for the kind of paid work Rua may eventually attract. He asked for this to be kept as strategic signal without architecture built around it.

### How he likes to work

Likes: sales, proposals, scoping, client calls, shooting, planning, and building systems and thinking about how the business could operate better.

Enjoys editing to an extent but gets bogged down in it. Editing and grading are where he consistently gets caught. Longer term he wants to spend less of his own time editing, whether through outsourcing, improved workflow, better systems around Final Cut Pro, or a combination.

By his own account he has not been spending enough time on bookkeeping, admin, scoping, proposals and particularly sales and lead generation.

He has never really tracked his time. Any hours-per-week figure he gives is a loose self-estimate and should be treated as such.

PRINCE2 trained, applied where useful and not dogmatically.

He resolves ambiguity by writing a definition and then working to it. This appears throughout his own documented rules and again in how he set the terms of this discovery.

Stated preference for output: practical, direct, concise, step-by-step over theory, no em dashes.

### How he uses AI in the work

Once an engagement progresses, calls are recorded and transcribed with AI, and he uses that material to generate the next iteration of the work: direction and pillars, then concepts, then the next stage towards the shoot. There is reusable machinery for this inside the Rua shoot skill, so delivery does not start from scratch each time.

He holds a firm and repeatedly stated line on how AI should behave inside his business: **architecture must be earned by demonstrated repeated friction.** He will reject a folder, skill, agent or permanent operating principle proposed off an inference or an apparent gap. He wants findings labelled by their evidential status, and strategic signals retained without being prematurely systematised. He asked for his answers to be used to test and revise a model rather than be fitted to one.

### Client relationships

He likes referral clients and says so directly. He liked working with Bríd, found Deirdre easy to deal with, and singles out Kasia as the kind of client relationship he wants more of.

He is willing to do significant thinking on a client's behalf where they do not know what they should be doing with content, as with Deirdre.

He identified a repeatable creative lesson from Deirdre: for clients uncomfortable presenting to camera, do not force the talking-head format. Use customer testimonials, B-roll and separately recorded voiceover.

### Commercial preferences

Terms are 50% upfront and 50% on delivery of final files, written into the scope of work. Applied consistently and currently enforced: Deirdre, Skehans and 3Dental all paid 50% upfront, and no Fitzpatricks shoot date has been confirmed because the deposit is unpaid. The deposit is what moves an engagement from sales and pre-production discussion into confirmed work.

He does substantial research, direction-building and creative thinking before the deposit, deliberately, so he has enough command of the problem to lead the sales and discovery calls properly. He distinguishes this from starting contracted production for free.

There is no single standard proposal artefact. He generally presents rather than sending a document: relevant work, the problem or opportunity, then pricing. The Fitzpatricks structure, which he rated as working well, showed retainer options first (edit-only, a middle option around €2,200 a month built around roughly one shoot plus output, a higher-volume option around €4,000 a month), with the content sprint then landing as a relatively straightforward entry point.

He designs offers with a deliverable floor below what he expects to produce. Skehans was scoped at around ten concepts as a floor and possibly fifteen guaranteed, while he internally expected closer to twenty-five.

He has identified two structurally different commercial lines: Rua-led engagements where Rua originates the client, develops strategy and concepts and delivers the whole engagement, and subcontract or pickup work through Fearghal where the client and often the concepts are already supplied. He stated that the commercial model for the second, along with day rates, shoot-only and edit-only, is not properly worked out, and that the Fearghal work exposed that gap.

### Systems and tools

He wants to keep developing the organisational systems and software and tools around Rua, potentially as useful personal or business software, without turning Rua into a large organisation. Stated as an interest, with no defined form.

### Stated weaknesses, anxieties and tendencies

These are his words and should be preserved at his level of certainty, not upgraded.

- "I'm not good enough at putting myself out there." He said this appears both in the lack of proactive sales and lead generation for Rua and in how he approaches promoting his music. In the verification pass he judged "it has never really been necessary" to read truer than fear of exposure, while allowing that "there may still be avoidance or perfectionism involved, particularly around consistency."
- Part of the problem, in his account, is trying to perfect things rather than getting them out into the world. Cited about the website and case studies.
- The absence of a reliable source of sales causes him a lot of anxiety, because no system currently creates opportunities predictably.
- On 3Dental he attributed the grading overrun largely to his own production choices made without a sufficiently tested system, while also noting the client did not hold to their side of the agreed turnaround.
- On Fearghal he judged that he had been ahead of himself on pricing, caused unnecessary friction, reduced the price, and thought that was fair.

## Level 2. Observed patterns supported by evidence

Narrowly phrased. Each is an observation across multiple examples, not a stated fact.

**Referral-dependent, not referral-only.** Every engagement discussed arrived through a person: his father Ray Hoare for 3Dental, Bríd for Deirdre, Paul Ryan at Otel for Fitzpatricks, Adam for Skehans, Fearghal for Family Grass and Ecoplex. But he also originated at least one route himself, shooting free videos at a market, which produced the introduction to Bríd and then the Deirdre engagement. That route worked and has not been repeated. The accurate pattern is dependence with a demonstrated exception, not incapacity.

**Price is set with the relationship's future in view.** 3Dental, Skehans, Fitzpatricks and Deirdre were each rationalised partly as a foot in the door or a relationship investment, with strategic value in the case study and the ongoing relationship named explicitly for Fitzpatricks. This is a consistent and deliberate approach rather than an accident, and he has articulated the reasoning each time. What is observable is that the same rationale has now applied to four consecutive Rua-led jobs and that he has separately said he would price several of them higher again.

**Scope has expanded by choice on at least one significant job, as an explicit experiment.** Skehans went from a priced one-day shoot to two days because he chose to let it grow, to do the work properly and to test what the relationship could produce. He also brought Sean Browne over to assist. Those costs did not appear in that price. This is documented on one job. It should not be generalised into a standing costing failure across the business, and the choice itself was deliberate.

**He under-promises deliverables and expects to exceed them.** Visible in the Skehans offer design and consistent with the floor, landed and ceiling logic in his own documented delivery thinking.

**Rework concentrates at the boundary between shoot and post.** The 3Dental grading overrun came from unmatched cameras and untested decisions made on location. His own craft ambition is phrased as making decisions in advance rather than rescuing things in the edit. The friction and the ambition describe the same boundary from opposite sides. One strongly evidenced instance, plus a stated general tendency to get bogged down in editing and grading.

**Sales cycles run long with no follow-up cadence.** Fitzpatricks roughly four to five months and open, Skehans February to June, Deirdre roughly a month to close and another to shoot. Consistent across cases, with no stated mechanism for advancing them.

**Client-facing output ships; Rua-facing output does not, consistently.** Four dated concept iterations, a nine-page handover and delivered sprints on the client side. On the Rua side, a Framer site that is not current and no published case studies from a completed sprint. Set against that, he has posted publicly on LinkedIn, including work about three weeks before the discovery, and sustained a four-month public campaign for Papa Rua. The pattern is inconsistency in self-directed output, not an inability to publish.

**Available capacity appears to exceed committed client work, but the size is unknown.** He has described periods of low demand and named the absence of leads rather than the absence of time as the reason the sprint model has not been rerun. He has never tracked his time, and his estimates exclude unpaid research, thinking and pre-deposit work, which he describes as substantial. The direction is supported; the quantity is not.

## Level 3. Open and uncertain

Founder-level questions that genuinely matter and remain unresolved.

**Whether "not putting myself out there" is a stable trait, a consistency problem, a necessity problem, or a mixture.** He stated the tendency. He also produced four months of sustained public output for Papa Rua and posts on LinkedIn irregularly. He favours the necessity reading while leaving avoidance and perfectionism open, particularly around consistency. His stated preference is to test the behaviour rather than explain it psychologically in advance. This should stay open.

**How representative any hours-per-week figure is.** He has never tracked his time. Estimates exclude pre-deposit research, direction-building and creative thinking, which he does deliberately and describes as substantial. Neither his working load nor his real spare capacity is currently known.

**What Otel actually represented.** It is referenced throughout as the loss that has not been absorbed, but nothing in the discovery establishes what it was worth, how long it ran, what the work involved, or why it ended. It also produced the Fitzpatricks introduction and the Benjamin Aguilera collaboration. The size of the hole it left is asserted rather than evidenced.

**How much the music-video and music-content direction should eventually influence Rua's commercial identity.** He does not know whether it is commercially viable, and said so. He wants it retained and tested, not discarded for lacking a workflow and not turned into positioning prematurely. Whether it is a business direction or a preference that shapes which jobs he accepts is deliberately unsettled.

**Whether the systems and software intent stays internal tooling or becomes something more.** Stated as an interest in developing useful personal or business software around Rua, with no defined form, no commercial intent expressed, and no timeline.

**Whether the lean model and the income target are compatible.** A small number of high-value engagements at the prices charged so far sits close to his comfort number with little room for production inputs. The prices that would make it work have not been set.

**What "spend less time editing" resolves to.** Outsourcing, workflow, better systems around Final Cut. All three named, none chosen, and the options have different cost implications for a lean business.

**Whether the sprint model repeats.** Skehans is the only complete instance. Fitzpatricks has validated the sales proposition but not delivery, and he is explicit that it should not count as proof until it runs. He attributes the lack of testing to insufficient leads rather than to a problem with the offer, and that attribution is untested.

## What an AI inside Rua should explicitly avoid assuming

- Do not assume growth intent. More clients, more revenue, more headcount and more hours are not defaults here. Volume is close to an anti-goal.
- Do not assume an absent system is a problem to solve. Absence is a finding. Architecture is earned by demonstrated repeated friction, and proposals built off an inference or a gap will be rejected.
- Do not assume Rua exists to fund music from a distance. The stated intent is overlap between paid work and the craft he wants to develop.
- Do not assume "spend less time editing" means he dislikes craft. He wants more craft, earlier in the process.
- Do not assume he avoids or dislikes selling. He likes sales, proposals, scoping and client calls. Any gap is in origination, not in selling.
- Do not upgrade "I'm not good enough at putting myself out there" into a proven psychological trait. It is his self-report, partially contradicted by his own behaviour, and he has asked for it to be tested rather than assumed.
- Do not infer fear of exposure from inconsistent publishing.
- Do not treat foot-in-the-door pricing as pathological or accidental. It has been deliberate and reasoned each time.
- Do not universalise the Skehans production-costing issue. It is one job, and the scope expansion on it was a chosen experiment and relationship investment.
- Do not treat any hours-per-week figure as tracked data or as a definitive measure of spare capacity.
- Do not treat "referral-only" as accurate. Referral-dependent, with one demonstrated self-originated route.
- Do not treat the €3,000 to €4,000 figure as a Rua revenue target. It is personal comfort.
- Do not treat Skehans as proof that the sprint model repeats.
- Do not treat the music-video direction as a decided positioning, and do not discard it either.
- Do not convert evidence into tasks. The specific jobs, invoices, contacts and dates gathered in this discovery are evidence for this model, not a to-do list.

## Addendum, August 2026: closed analyses and candidate direction

The design work that followed the closed analyses below identified **defined creative ownership** as the leading candidate operating direction: clearly defined, paid creative projects where the client brings the business need and retains the final decision, while Rua owns the creative response and production of a defined body of work with a clear finish. Darragh found it attractive but did not adopt it, and subsequently clarified it may be too early to treat as settled. It is recorded in `founder-context.md` as a candidate only, not policy. The underlying analyses, all closed:

**Qualitative synthesis (13 August 2026).** The canonical qualitative operating-model synthesis. Closed.

**Minimum financial verification.** Completed and closed; it did not materially contradict the synthesis. It established only the broad shape: recorded net sales of approximately €35.6k; the largest directly coded client at approximately 73.2% of recorded net sales, at most 78.5% under the broader supported attribution; logged net outlays of approximately €16.3k, of which approximately €9.35k was equipment-related; the workbook's approximately €19.35k indicative surplus is not a P&L. Owner hours, effective rates and engagement profitability remain unknown because the source evidence does not contain that grain. Do not rebuild the accounts, reconstruct time, allocate historical overhead or calculate invented engagement margins. The Otel deposit reconciliation is closed; do not revisit it.

**Blind-first and named-audit robustness exercise.** Found no material reason to believe client names or sectors were carrying the strategic conclusion. Its blind answer initially mixed three separate layers, engagement responsibility, origination mechanism, and portfolio and commercial guardrails; that category error was corrected. Acquisition channels and concentration rules are not themselves complete operating models.
